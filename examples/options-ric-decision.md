## Executive Recommendation

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Narrow Platform (Phase 1 Focus)

Nippon Light Metal (NLM) should pursue the Narrow Platform strategy, positioning Nikkei Engineering as the essential "triage" middleware between citizen reporting and professional repair. This approach secures a scalable recurring revenue stream and leverages NLM's engineering licensure to validate data, while mitigating the high capital risks and legal barriers associated with a full end-to-end marketplace.

### Decision Rationale

This option is recommended based on the following analysis:

#### 1. Alignment with Success Metrics

- **For Nippon Light Metal / Nikkei Engineering**: This option establishes a new profit center by monetizing the "triage" step—converting raw data into engineering specifications. It creates recurring subscription revenue through B2G contracts for "Cluster Management" services. Crucially, it strengthens Nikkei Engineering's strategic position by using its legal licensure to act as the gatekeeper of infrastructure data, preventing commoditization by pure software competitors \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].
- **For Local Governments**: The "Concierge MVP" model directly addresses staff shortages by outsourcing the validation of citizen reports. By filtering noise and prioritizing defects before they reach municipal desks, this option accelerates the detection-to-repair cycle and ensures data continuity without requiring new government staff \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].

#### 2. Risk-Adjusted Assessment

- **Risk Profile**: Option 2 presents a medium risk profile, significantly lower than the "massive capital" and legal exposure of Option 3. The primary risk is procurement rigidity (municipal ability to pay for subscriptions), which is manageable compared to the existential legal risks of the full platform.
- **Acceptable Risk**: The investment required is moderate, primarily for a small product team and operational staff. This cost is justified by the potential to secure a defensive moat around NLM's core engineering business.
- **De-riskability**: This option is highly de-riskable. NLM can validate the business model through a "Concierge MVP" (manual validation) within months. If municipalities refuse to pay for the service during the pilot phase, NLM can exit with minimal sunk costs, unlike the multi-year development required for Option 3 \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].

#### 3. Decision Context Fit

This recommendation aligns with the "Go – Narrow Platform" pathway. It acknowledges that NLM lacks the native software capabilities to build a consumer-grade application from scratch. By partnering with Dappi Studio (FixMyStreet) for data intake (Step 1) and focusing NLM's resources on Inspection and Planning (Steps 2–3), the strategy plays to the company's "Category A" strengths in engineering analysis and licensure while outsourcing the consumer interface \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].

### Why Other Options Were Not Selected

#### Option 1: No-Go / Status Quo

**Not Recommended Because**:

- It exposes NLM to significant strategic risk of obsolescence. As competitors like Nippon Koei digitize, NLM risks becoming a commoditized subcontractor with no control over the data ecosystem \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].
- It fails to achieve the corporate goal of establishing a new, scalable profit center, leaving the company entirely dependent on cyclical construction bids.

**When This Option Would Be Preferred**:

- If NLM faces an immediate liquidity crisis requiring absolute cash preservation.
- If MLIT explicitly mandates a single government-run platform that excludes private sector middleware.

#### Option 3: Full End-to-End Platform

**Not Recommended Because**:

- It faces a severe legal barrier regarding the "separation of design and construction duties." Japanese law restricts the same entity from designing repairs and managing construction bids, making an integrated marketplace legally inviable \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].
- It requires "massive capital" and a development timeline exceeding 3.5 years, which creates a payback period that likely fails internal financial hurdles.

**When This Option Would Be Preferred**:

- If Japanese construction laws are amended to allow design-build integration within digital platforms.
- If NLM acquires a major software company with existing marketplace infrastructure and user base.

### Critical Next Steps

If the recommended option is pursued, these are the immediate priorities:

1. **Partnership Formalization**: Sign a binding agreement with Dappi Studio (FixMyStreet) to secure data access and define API integration terms \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].
2. **Concierge MVP Launch**: Deploy a manual operations team to process citizen reports for a pilot municipality, validating the workflow before writing software code.
3. **Procurement Legal Review**: Commission a legal opinion to confirm that "Cluster Management" services can be paid for via municipal OpEx budgets rather than CapEx construction bonds.
4. **Pilot Acquisition**: Secure one "Gun-Mane" policy zone municipality for a paid pilot to prove willingness-to-pay for the triage service.
5. **Termination of Legacy R&D**: Halt all internal R&D related to full marketplace development to reallocate resources to the middleware solution \[CHAPTER: three-options-detailed\].

### Recommendation Confidence

**Confidence Level**: High

**Factors Supporting Confidence**:

- **Asset Fit**: NLM's engineering licensure provides a unique competitive advantage that pure software companies cannot replicate.
- **Market Demand**: Aging infrastructure and labor shortages create an urgent, quantified need for efficiency tools in local government.

**Factors Reducing Confidence**:

- **Procurement Friction**: Uncertainty remains regarding whether municipalities can legally pay for subscription services under current budget rules.
- **Partner Dependency**: Reliance on Dappi Studio for the data funnel introduces third-party risk.

**What Would Change This Recommendation**:

- **Regulatory Block**: If legal review confirms municipalities cannot pay for "Cluster Management" services separate from construction contracts.
- **Partner Refusal**: If Dappi Studio refuses to partner or demands prohibitive revenue sharing, NLM would need to reassess the cost of building its own intake tool.
